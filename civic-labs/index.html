SPCC.1 / Civic Labs

Public questions. Real places. Shared work.

Civic Labs help communities use the public institutions, relationships, and knowledge they already have to investigate consequential questions, create rigorous learning, and produce useful public work.

A living civic inheritance

The strands already exist. The opportunity is to braid them.

Across the country, people have built schools as community institutions, deeper-learning networks around real-world work, universities around public purpose, and civic traditions around shared responsibility. Civic Labs bring those strands into relationship in a real place.

Community Schools

Schools as neighborhood institutions connected to families, public agencies, health, food, housing, and local organizations.

Deeper Learning

Inquiry, interdisciplinary projects, internships, performance, and public demonstrations that connect learning to life beyond school.

Public Institutions

Universities, libraries, agencies, community colleges, and civic organizations contributing knowledge, space, relationships, and reach.

Economic Democracy

Questions of ownership, governance, public investment, shared stewardship, and how communities build assets that last.

What would it look like if these traditions could work together?

A Civic Lab creates a place where students, educators, institutions, and community partners can investigate a question that matters and make something useful together.

The work is not to invent a new system from scratch. It is to grow the capacity already present in a place.

What Civic Labs make possible

A structure for turning a real community question into shared work.

Civic Labs use public education as a common platform. They connect curriculum, educators, students, community partners, universities, public agencies, and local resources around work that matters.

01 · Place

Begin with a question that belongs to a community.

Students and partners investigate an issue they can see, study, and discuss with people who live with its consequences.

02 · Practice

Support educators in making the work rigorous.

Teachers need shared structures, planning time, examples, coaching, and a community of practice.

03 · Contribution

Produce something useful for a real audience.

Learning becomes public work: a brief, map, recommendation, presentation, tool, or decision.

04 · Capacity

Leave the place stronger than before.

The work preserves knowledge, relationships, and a next step that others can carry forward.

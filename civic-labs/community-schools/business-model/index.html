The business model

Service creates the transaction. Product preserves its meaning.

Civic Labs organize authentic work among learners, schools, communities, and institutions. The Community Learner Ledger records that work only after an independent public or private entity confirms that it happened, mattered, and meets an agreed standard.

Service

Civic Labs + Community Schools

Field-building, partner alignment, public questions, authentic projects, facilitation, demonstration, and institutional validation.

→
Product

Community Learner Ledger

A licensed protocol that pairs contribution with outside validation and issues a portable, stackable credential. SPCC.1 owns the protocol; participants retain their data.

→
Market

Institutional adoption

Government, business, higher education, and philanthropy pay for the community infrastructure, knowledge, authenticity, and legitimacy they use—not for a favorable validation.

A two-sided marketplace with reciprocal roles. On one side, people, communities, and institutions contribute knowledge, work, relationships, evidence, and trust. On the other, institutions receive, validate, credential, purchase, and act on that value. The sides are not fixed: a university, government agency, foundation, nonprofit, or business may contribute in one transaction and purchase or validate in another. Everyone needs the infrastructure; everyone benefits from a stronger field; and everyone pays when they use value the field created.

The regenerative mechanism

What is the Civic Tax—and why is it necessary?

Institutions routinely depend on knowledge, trust, relationships, participation, natural systems, and legitimacy they did not create. They invite communities into planning processes, use their stories and lived experience, ask them to validate strategies, and rely on their participation to make institutional decisions credible. Most of that value is never priced, recorded, or returned to the people and places that produced it.

The Civic Tax is a required allocation on an institutional payment for using that shared civic value. It is not a tax on learners, communities, or frontline organizations. It is paid by the institution reaching into the field. The payment compensates contribution now, maintains the infrastructure that made the exchange possible, and leaves behind a permanent community asset.

P · People

Learners, educators, residents, practitioners, organizers, and community institutions contribute work, knowledge, creativity, relationships, lived experience, and trust.

The ledger records

Who contributed, what they contributed, who received it, and how its value was recognized. People retain their data and learners receive the credential.

P · Planet

Land, water, energy, ecosystems, and natural resources make every community and economic activity possible, yet their contribution and depletion are usually absent from the transaction.

The ledger records

What natural systems made possible, and whether the work used, protected, restored, or harmed them. Regenerative value can be directed back to place.

P · Public

Public schools, districts, colleges, universities, cities, agencies, and other government institutions can receive, validate, credential, purchase, and act on community contribution.

The institutional obligation

When a public institution uses community learning, participation, authenticity, or legitimacy, it pays for that value and validates what it received.

P · Private

Businesses, employers, nonprofits, foundations, private universities, and philanthropy can receive, validate, purchase, invest in, and benefit from community contribution.

The institutional obligation

Private status does not excuse extraction. When a private institution uses community value, it pays the Civic Tax and makes its use visible.

People and Planet create value. Public and Private institutions validate, use, and finance it. The ledger makes the exchange visible; the Civic Tax makes the exchange reciprocal.

Where the Civic Tax enters the transaction

Catalytic grants can build and prove the infrastructure. The Civic Tax begins when a public or private institution—including a foundation, college, or university—pays for Civic Labs, validation pathways, community intelligence, participation, authenticity, or legitimacy. A foundation grant may therefore occupy either category: catalytic capital when it underwrites shared development risk, or a Civic Tax transaction when it commissions or uses community value.

10%Community corpus

A protected, permanent balance-sheet asset associated with the community whose value was used.

10%Shared infrastructure

Maintains the protocol, ledger, field, standards, licenses, and institutional network operated by SPCC.1.

80%Contributors + production

Pays the people and organizations that produced the value: field builders, local operators, community partners, educators, practitioners, learner supports, and Civic Lab delivery.

1 · DemandA government, business, university, or foundation needs community knowledge, participation, learning, or legitimacy.
2 · PurchaseIt pays for the Civic Lab service and licensed ledger infrastructure.
3 · VerificationAuthentic work is independently validated and credentials are issued.
4 · ReturnThe Civic Tax funds contributors, infrastructure, and community corpus.
5 · RenewalInstitutional value drives another purchase; corpus and field capacity compound.
A validating institution never pays for a favorable judgment. It pays for the community value it uses and for the infrastructure and process that make an independent judgment possible.

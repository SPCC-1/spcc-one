The business model

The method produces verified contribution. The product makes it ownable, usable, and cumulative.

Civic Labs use the Civic Method to organize rigorous public work among learners, schools, communities, and institutions. The Community Learning Ledger records the evidence, authorship, quality, validation, and public value of that work. Together, the method and product create civic infrastructure.

Service

Civic Labs + Community Schools

Field-building, partner alignment, public questions, authentic projects, facilitation, demonstration, and institutional validation.

→
Product

Community Learning Ledger

A licensed protocol that pairs contribution with outside validation, preserves community assets, and issues portable evidence to the learner.

→
Market

Institutional adoption

Government, business, higher education, and philanthropy pay for the community infrastructure, knowledge, authenticity, and legitimacy they use—not for a favorable validation.

Supply side · Creators and owners

Young people, schools, and communities

  • Bring live questions, lived knowledge, relationships, learning time, and creative work.
  • Produce research, recommendations, designs, stories, datasets, and other community assets.
  • Learners own their portable evidence; communities retain the public assets they helped create.
Verified contribution
Demand side · Users and payers

Public, private, philanthropic, and educational institutions

  • Bring consequential questions, standards, expertise, validation, pathways, and purchasing power.
  • Use trustworthy community knowledge, participation, talent, and public legitimacy.
  • Pay for the method, ledger, delivery, access, and value they use—not for a favorable judgment.

The marketplace connects the sides; it does not collapse their roles. Independent review protects rigor and trust. The unit that moves across the market is a verified contribution with evidence and provenance—not a credential or endorsement for sale.

The exchange is reciprocal. Institutions gain useful, credible community assets and access to demonstrated talent. Learners gain recognition and pathways. Communities retain knowledge and capacity. Revenue sustains delivery, compensates contributors, maintains the ledger, and helps finance the next cycle.
Phase I · Demonstration

One site. One complete Civic Lab. One working model.

Establish the partnership, deliver the Civic Method in a real community-school setting, and complete the first end-to-end verified contribution with a usable ledger record.

$375KSite + working-model capital

Establish the demonstration site

Align one Community School, community partners, young people, educators, a live public question, an authentic audience, and an independent validation pathway.

Gate: every participant understands the question, roles, standards, ownership, and intended public value.

Deliver the complete Civic Method

Frame the question, investigate evidence and lived knowledge, create useful public work, and demonstrate it before a real audience.

Gate: the site completes one rigorous cycle from community question to public demonstration.

Prove the working model

Record authorship, evidence, quality, external judgment, learner recognition, and the community asset in the minimum viable ledger.

Gate: one complete verified contribution produces both public value and portable learning evidence.

Phase II · Product development + next degree of scale

Turn the working model into a usable product and a repeatable network.

Develop the Community Learning Ledger beyond its minimum viable form, extend delivery to multiple sites, and prove that the method, standards, and ownership model can operate beyond the original demonstration.

$500KProduct + replication capital

Develop the ledger product

Build evidence capture, permissions, provenance, countersignature, asset ownership, audit trail, credential issuance, portability, and institutional reporting.

Gate: schools, communities, learners, validators, and purchasers can use the product with clear rights and responsibilities.

Reach the next degree of scale

Deliver across a small network of sites, train additional operators, test distinct entry questions, and maintain consistent standards across settings.

Gate: the method and product work beyond one site and without depending on founder-led delivery alone.

Prove adoption and willingness to pay

Document learner and community value, refine pricing and implementation, and secure renewals, expansion commitments, or procurement pathways.

Gate: at least one institutional user commits earned revenue to continued use or expansion.

Phase III · Go to market

Build the paying market.

Move from a repeatable network to civic infrastructure purchased by every class of institution that uses community knowledge, verified contribution, learning pathways, and public legitimacy: government, business, higher education, and philanthropy.

$2MMarket formation + adoption

Expand to a multi-site operating network

Deliver across multiple schools and communities, train local operators, establish regional field stewards, and create a durable network of public and private validators.

Gate: the service and product operate beyond a single site and beyond the founder alone.

Cross into customer-funded operations

Establish district, agency, university, employer, foundation, and intermediary procurement; standardize onboarding and licenses; integrate with learning, philanthropic, civic, and workforce systems.

Gate: institutional earned revenue covers ongoing delivery and infrastructure costs.

Build a durable renewal loop

Use recurring contracts and licensing revenue to sustain Civic Labs, compensate local partners, maintain the shared infrastructure, and make the next cycle of public work possible.

Gate: each completed cycle creates the demand, relationships, and revenue needed for the next one.

3. Universal Sovereignty

Alfred
October 8, 2026 · 11:50 AM

First, teachers and students are not just in a formal classroom. We are all teachers and students, aren’t we, throughout our lives. Informal learning and sharing is just as important as formal learning environments.

I have not been in school since 1997 when I earned my Master’s Degree in Urban Education at Mercy College in the Bronx. I think most of my learning has come as an adult. I’ve been married twice. I have two kids. I have lived in NYC, Pasadena, the East Bay, and now Plano/Dallas. I have made the shift from teaching to entrepreneurial ecosystem building, from being a public employee to a nonprofit leader to a business owner.

Learning, adaptation, and growth is the common denominator. I want young people and educators to understand that your identity as a learner, your capacity to grow and learn from your mistakes, to start over with a beginner’s mind and a humble heart, is your true identity.

Growth, change, becoming is why we are here. And what got us here is not going to get us to where we need to go next. We need to embrace the learner’s journey, to hit our ceiling, and begin again.

I discovered sovereign wealth and continually return to it because I believe that economic security is necessary for political participation. At a more basic level, we all have material needs. If we are in survival mode, we cannot experience love and belonging, and we cannot develop human flourishing.

I believe that we are made to be sovereign. We were literally made for freedom and human flourishing. We are in the process of making a shift from monarchies and extremely top-down hierarchies, where a small group of people has all of the power and control and human agency and freedom are extracted and flow upward, toward universal sovereignty, where freedom and agency are broadly distributed.

Technology can be used to maintain the status quo and capture value, or it can be a tool to liberate us. For me, capital and technology are tools. They are infrastructure. They are not the purpose of society. People are the purpose. Human flourishing is the purpose.

Alfred
October 8, 2026 · 11:50 AM

Why sovereign wealth funds?

  • Because we now have the tools to create economic security for the many.
  • We don’t need elites to create this for us.
  • We don’t need the government to do it for us.
  • We must do it for ourselves.
  • We need to understand how capital works.
  • We need to understand the power of compounding interest.
  • We need to learn how to steward resources.
  • We all need asset ownership.

Productive assets compound faster than wages. Wages and income are hand-to-mouth. A productive asset grows over time and can produce dividends, or you can borrow against it, which is non-taxable income to live off of.

Most people do not understand capital. They live on profit and loss, a checking account, and they are competing with inflation.

I don’t want to live in a world where human labor is not necessary. But I don’t want human labor on the plantation model of American capitalism. I want our labor to produce economic security, and that security to unlock human flourishing.

We are now $40 trillion in debt, and interest payments of $1 trillion and counting are going to crowd out our ability to pay for healthcare and education. Our children are going to have to pay this debt, and they will not have the same flexibility and social safety net that our parents have.

We will have to pay more and receive less. That’s the treadmill we are on.

I don’t want revolution and I don’t want the status quo. I want reform. I want to embrace capital for the benefit of community.

I think that may be the beginning of the book: helping people understand the system they are living in, helping them see that ownership is possible, and helping them imagine a future where capital and technology serve people rather than the other way around.

5. Making Wealth Building Possible and Making It the Norm

Salim
October 8, 2026 · 12:51 PM

I think you are trying to figure out two distinct things:

  • How to make wealth building possible.
  • How to make it the norm.

Politics can help with creating the first part of the equation. Teachers can play a large role in helping people think about the second part of that equation. Institutions can bridge the gap between them.

The government can create a 529 savings plan, but can’t make people enroll their kids in it. A good teacher like you can teach kids about 529 savings plans, but can’t make their parents enroll in it.

A hospital, however, could theoretically incorporate enrollment into the birth registration process, making it easy for every newborn to receive an investment account before leaving the hospital.

If this is the case, then maybe there is a way for public schools to take on that responsibility. That’s worth exploring, in my opinion.

Alfred
October 8, 2026 · 2:13 PM

Thanks for sharing the K2C program in San Francisco. NYC Kids RISE is the New York version. Both are publicly supported universal savings and asset-building programs.

In NYC there are 342,000 accounts and $57 million invested, or about $167 per account. In San Francisco there are 57,000 accounts and $19.6 million invested, or about $344 per account.

The ideas of Baby Bonds and asset building have been widely popular since an academic named Michael Sherraden wrote a book, Assets and the Poor: A New American Welfare Policy, which explained that income helps people consume and survive, while assets help people participate in the future.

He proposed Individual Development Accounts: publicly matched savings accounts for education, homeownership, business development, and retirement.

And nearly 40 years later, the top 10% of Americans have $50 trillion in retirement accounts and the poor have a few hundred each.

Something is not working. Something is very broken. And I believe it is the requirement that the accounts be individual.

Still, there is a missing mechanism: aggregation and compounding.

The key is that everyone participates and everyone benefits. Universal is how it withstands the pressure of partisan politics. If only one group benefits, or one group pays, it can be clawed back.

But once we all pay, and we all benefit, and if it is self-sustaining, and aligns overall economic growth with individual economic security, we have a New Deal for the Digital Economy of the 21st Century.
